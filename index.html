About Colorectal Surgery at Advitya Healthcares

Colorectal surgery at Advitya Healthcares is delivered within a gastrointestinal surgical practice experienced in complex abdominal conditions. The team evaluates and treats conditions affecting the colon, rectum and anus — including colon and rectal cancer, polyps, diverticular disease, complications of inflammatory bowel disease, rectal prolapse and anorectal conditions — with consultations available in Kolkata and Baruipur.

Your symptoms, examination findings, previous surgeries and available reports are all considered before treatment is planned. Whether a condition is managed medically or surgically — and whether an open, laparoscopic or minimally invasive approach is suitable — is decided after clinical evaluation and, where required, endoscopy, imaging and pathology.

The surgical team is led by Dr. Deeksha Kapoor, a pancreato-biliary and gastrointestinal surgeon with training in advanced surgical oncology and minimally invasive surgery from institutions in India, Japan, Germany and France. She leads the PancreaCare+ programme at Advitya Healthcares.

Before surgery you are told what the procedure involves, what anaesthesia is planned and what recovery looks like. After surgery you receive specific advice on diet, activity, wound care and follow-up. Heavy rectal bleeding, severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting or an inability to pass stool or wind needs emergency assessment the same day.

Common Symptoms of Colorectal Conditions

01

Blood in Stool

Blood during or after bowel movements may require medical evaluation.

Symptoms that settle and return, or that gradually worsen over several weeks, should be reviewed by a specialist.

Persistent blood in stool, unexplained weight loss, significant changes in bowel habits, persistent abdominal pain or other concerning symptoms should be evaluated by a qualified healthcare professional.

Patient education

What Is Colorectal Surgery?

Colorectal surgery is a specialised field of surgery focused on conditions affecting the colon, rectum and anus.

The part of the body involved

The colon, rectum and anus form the final part of the digestive tract. Together they absorb water from digested food, store waste and control its passage out of the body. Disease anywhere along this pathway can change bowel habit, cause bleeding or produce pain.

When colorectal surgery may be required

Surgery is one part of colorectal care, and it may be considered for:

  • Cancer
  • Complicated inflammatory conditions
  • Diverticular disease
  • Polyps
  • Rectal prolapse
  • Certain anorectal conditions
  • Other structural or surgical conditions

Why evaluation comes first

Many of these conditions share similar symptoms, so an accurate diagnosis matters before any treatment is planned. Examination, endoscopy, imaging and pathology each contribute to understanding what is causing the symptoms and whether an operation has a role.

The recommended treatment depends on the diagnosis, severity, overall health and individual clinical circumstances.

Treatment

Surgical Treatment Options

Several surgical approaches are available. Which one suits you is a clinical decision made after evaluation.

Approach 01

Laparoscopic Colorectal Surgery

Laparoscopic surgery uses small incisions and specialised instruments. Its suitability depends on the condition, planned procedure, previous surgery and the patient’s overall health.

  • Carried out through small incisions, viewed on a screen.
  • Previous abdominal surgery is taken into account.
  • Fitness for anaesthesia is assessed beforehand.
Approach 02

Open Colorectal Surgery

Open surgery uses a larger incision and may be appropriate in selected situations depending on the diagnosis and complexity of the procedure.

  • Direct access to the affected part of the colon or rectum.
  • May suit cases where scarring or complexity is a factor.
  • Preparation and recovery are explained beforehand.
Approach 03

Minimally Invasive Approaches

Selected colorectal procedures may be performed using minimally invasive techniques when this is clinically appropriate for the condition and the patient.

  • Considered only where the diagnosis and procedure allow it.
  • Not suitable for every condition or every patient.
  • Discussed with you along with the alternatives.

The surgical approach is determined after detailed clinical evaluation.
